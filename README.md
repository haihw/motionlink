# MotionLink

A native iPhone + Mac app pair. The iPhone streams fused gyroscope and accelerometer data; the Mac displays a 3D phone that follows its orientation in real time, with gyroscope rings, direction highlights, acceleration vectors, estimated movement speed, and phone-controlled sword, wok cooking, drawing, laser maze, and paper plane flight demos.

## Run the apps

1. Open `MotionLink.xcodeproj` in Xcode 26 or newer.
2. Select **MotionLinkMac**, choose **My Mac**, and run.
3. Select **MotionLinkPhone**. In **Signing & Capabilities**, select your Apple development team and use a unique bundle identifier if necessary. Choose your physical iPhone and run. Enable Developer Mode on the iPhone if prompted.
4. Put both devices on the same Wi-Fi network. Allow **Local Network** access on each device and motion access if requested.
5. On the iPhone, enter the six-digit code shown on the Mac, then select your Mac.
6. Hold the phone upright with its screen facing you. Hold it still and select **Confirm neutral position** in Sword Demo, or **Set neutral pose** in Motion Studio / the phone app. Tilt, turn, and move the phone to see the gyro rings, arrows, and live movement readings.

Minimum versions: **iOS 17** and **macOS 14**. No backend, account, third-party libraries, or external 3D assets are required.

The Mac's **Try demo** button animates rotation and a known synthetic movement trajectory, including acceleration, speed, and braking arrows, without a connected phone. The iOS Simulator can exercise discovery and pairing, but it has no real motion sensors. Use a physical iPhone for sensor testing.

## Sword Demo

Select **Sword Demo** in the Mac app (it opens by default). Pair your existing phone app, hold it still and upright with the screen facing you, then click **Confirm neutral position**. The button becomes available after at least 0.6 seconds of upright, quiet sensor readings.

- Moving your hand translates the grip left/right, up/down, and toward/away from you, with 3× sensitivity. Position is integrated from the short-gesture velocity estimate on the phone sensor clock and follows the phone as a sword handle, with the blade extending along its top edge. After 0.8 seconds of quiet acceleration and rotation at any angle, the grip smoothly recenters without changing its angle. Movement immediately interrupts recentering; automatic centering cannot score a slash.
- All three attitude axes rotate the sword directly. Tilt the phone forward to point the blade into the arena, tilt backward to point toward you, and combine pitch, yaw, and roll to aim and slash. Neutral points the sword upright.
- **Reset neutral** pauses slicing and starts setup again. Confirm your comfortable upright grip before continuing. Reconnection, stale input, pause/resume, and calibration also require a new confirmation. The sensor checks upright posture and steadiness; you confirm that the screen faces you.
- Three melons hang at different heights and depths: **Crimson** has dark striped green skin and red flesh, **Golden** is larger with light skin and yellow flesh, and **Mini** is smaller with narrow stripes.
- Hits split the melon along the actual blade plane and contact offset into seeded pieces that tumble down with juice particles. Fast swings leave a short blade trail. Each fruit scores once; slicing all three completes the round.
- **Reset fruit** restores all three melons. **Watch demo** plays a scripted round through the same collision engine without requiring a phone. **Stop demo** returns control to your phone.
- **Motion Studio** remains available in the header selector. Leaving Sword Demo ends that round; entering it again creates a fresh round.

The sword preserves the full calibrated quaternion and adds estimated hand translation in the fixed neutral frame. Travel is bounded to ±1.35 horizontally, ±0.8 vertically, and ±1.1 in depth around the neutral grip. Inertial translation is approximate and may drift; **Reset neutral** recenters the grip and requires confirmation again. Speed resets also clear the grip displacement and collision sweep. A slash needs swing energy, blade movement, and edge-first contact. Flat-side pushes and straight thrusts do not slice. Swept oriented-blade collision testing catches contacts between frames and records the cutting plane in 3D; idle overlap, resetting neutral, paused input, and long frame gaps do not produce phantom hits.

Use the matching protocol-3 phone and Mac builds. Re-pair after updating or restarting the Mac viewer.

## Wok Tossing & Searing

Select **Wok Demo** in the Mac header. The phone is the wok: its screen corresponds to the bowl’s opening, and its tilt, rotation, and estimated translation drive the whole bowl. A turquoise rim mark corresponds to the phone’s top edge so heading changes are visible. Click **Start cooking session** (or **Restart cooking session** to clear the plate and scores). Hold the screen facing upward and roughly level with a relaxed hand. After a brief comfortable hold, a visible **3–2–1 countdown** automatically captures position and heading and cracks the egg, preserving the phone’s actual inclination to gravity. Every egg gets a freshly randomized **Soft**, **Medium**, or **Firm** request, displayed beside its cooking meter; repeats are possible. Later eggs use the same relaxed countdown without another click. Natural hand tremor is accepted; short disturbances pause the countdown, while sustained/large movement or stale samples reset it. This demo uses the existing motion stream and does not use the camera.

- Circle the phone on a horizontal plane to stir. The egg moves under world-space gravity, curved-bowl contact, static/kinetic friction, and moving-rim contact. It lags and orbits under sustained circular movement and settles when the bowl stops. Tilting slides it downhill; rotation turns the bowl. Large tilts or inversion spill the egg, which falls onto the counter.
- A quick forward push (away from you) followed by, or combined with, an upward lift triggers an assisted flip. The forward component must precede the lift by at most 0.3 seconds. The detector captures an 80 ms peak, so a faster push/lift or stronger impulse chooses **two full spins**, while a normal flick chooses **one full spin**. Both add a half-turn and land on the opposite face. The guided arc follows the moving wok and returns the egg to its center; this is the deliberate game exception to ordinary contact physics. A held gesture cannot repeatedly toss the egg; lower the motion to rearm.
- Toss gestures work at any cooking level. In Cook mode, the first-side green band (**50–72%**, or **60–72%** for Firm) awards a **Perfect flip**. Follow the randomly requested **Soft**, **Medium**, or **Firm** doneness for that egg. Once both faces reach their required levels, **a big push-forward-and-lift toss sends the egg onto the serving plate**. The same faster gesture that ordinarily produces a double spin becomes the serving throw; normal tosses still flip. The egg follows a higher assisted ballistic arc to the fixed plate, lands yolk-up, and completes the round only on the catch. Searing stops at launch. No Serve button is needed. Three stars require a perfect flip and serving within the green bands. Past-target eggs can still be served before burning, with a lower timing score. Early flips remain allowed; both faces must finish cooking before serving unlocks.
- Served eggs persist for the session. The plate supports **three staggered layers**, with spacing to clear the yolks. Further catches overhang the stack and tip/slide off the rim into gravity-driven counter contact; spilled eggs bounce, lose momentum to friction, and collide/pile against other spilled eggs. They retain their browning. Restarting clears every egg. The model uses flattened rigid-body contacts rather than deforming food or a fluid simulation.
- An egg resting against the wok for 0.20 seconds heats at **2× speed**. Sliding/stirring relative to the contact surface above 0.04 m/s returns it to normal speed. The HUD shows **Egg resting · heat ×2**. Cracking, airborne/serving phases, practice mode, and paused input add no heat.
- A **three-star egg** triggers a mint/gold confetti burst and “Perfect egg!” cheer at the catch. Reduce Motion suppresses flying confetti while retaining the static cheer.
- Enable **Practice · heat off** before starting for an unlimited motion playground. The egg is already set, searing does not advance, and you can explore circles, tilts, single spins, and double spins without a burn timer. **Watch demo** runs a repeated single/double-spin playground in Practice mode or a continuing session with random requests, persistent plated eggs, and overflow in Cook mode. Stop demo displays its session summary.
- Input pause, disconnection, or stale motion pauses the round. Slow renderer intervals are subdivided into bounded physics steps so a throttled window does not freeze cooking; large background gaps are capped. After a result, the game leaves a four-second break before preparing another automatic countdown. **Auto next egg · 3-second countdown** can be switched off to pause subsequent eggs; the Start button still arms the first egg. Watch demo remains available. A new countdown captures a fresh pose for the next egg. **Recenter wok** clears translation while preserving angle and the grounded egg’s relative position; it is disabled during a flip or serving flight. Estimated movement uses 1× scale within a free range, then smoothly resists outward movement. The wok stays within a circular **0.22 m radius** over the burner, can lift at most **0.12 m**, and cannot translate below the stove support. Resistance begins at 0.14 m horizontally and 0.07 m vertically. Tangential circular stirring and movement back toward the center remain responsive; no hidden displacement accumulates against the limits. Rotation and flick recognition continue at the limits. It holds its position when stopped; manual recentering corrects drift.
- Fold the phone downward **at any point in an active session** to stop, including setup, cracking, searing, flipping, and the serving throw. The detector accepts either the top edge down by more than 55° or the screen facing the floor, held for **0.20 seconds**; a fast fold also works. Short transients, sideways tilts, upright/level holds, stale samples, and timestamp gaps do not end it. An **End session** button offers the same action throughout the session. The summary includes the **unfinished egg as 0 stars**, alongside burnt/dropped eggs, in the average. Eggs already caught on the plate keep their score and model; stopping before the first egg starts adds no extra egg. Disconnecting pauses the active egg, which resumes after another setup hold.
- Leaving Wok Demo ends that session. All views share the same phone connection. Cooking levels are accelerated game values, not cooking times, temperatures, or food-safety measurements.

The bowl, ceramic serving plate, egg, cracked shells, burner, steam, and browning surfaces are programmatic RealityKit models. The egg is approximated as a rigid contact body; deformation and oil fluid dynamics are not modeled.

## Behavior

- Core Motion fuses the gyroscope and accelerometer using `xArbitraryZVertical` at a requested 60 Hz. Actual update frequency depends on the device and scheduling.
- The Mac applies relative quaternions directly to a RealityKit model. The model includes a screen, metal frame, buttons, and rear camera lenses.
- Motion Studio uses the first received pose as its display reference. Sword Demo requires an explicit neutral confirmation before scoring. **Set neutral pose** recalibrates without reconnecting, then requires sword setup again. Model axes are X right, Y toward the top of the phone, Z out of the screen.
- Three colored 3D gyroscope rings rotate with the phone and brighten with rotation or movement on each axis. The six signed direction axes highlight travel relative to the neutral pose.
- The **green arrow** shows short-gesture movement direction, preferring estimated velocity. The **amber arrow** shows acceleration. They can point in opposite directions when braking. Arrow length is a bounded visualization of magnitude, not physical displacement.
- Both apps display acceleration magnitude and components in **m/s²**, excluding gravity, in the neutral frame. **Estimated speed** is in **m/s**. **Reset speed** on either app assumes the phone is held still and resets both apps; setting the neutral pose also resets speed.
- The wire payload carries device acceleration in **g**. Gyroscope readings are in **radians/second**. Receive rate is measured on the Mac over one-second intervals, rather than inferred from the phone's clock.
- Pausing stops sensor updates and lets the display sleep. Resuming establishes a fresh neutral pose because Core Motion starts a new reference frame.
- The iPhone stays awake while streaming. Leaving it in the background stops motion and disconnects; return to the app and pair again with the new code.
- A Mac accepts one phone at a time. The pairing code changes on disconnection. After a network interruption, choose the Mac and pair again.
- The viewer holds the last received pose if updates stop and displays **Waiting for motion** after two seconds. A clean disconnection clears the readings and resets the model.

## Scope of motion tracking

Rotation comes directly from fused device attitude. Translation direction and speed are **short-movement estimates** based on acceleration; they are not measured ground-truth velocity or position. The 3D phone stays at the instrument origin, surrounded by arrows and highlighted directions.

The estimator rotates acceleration into a fixed neutral frame, converts g to m/s², smooths it over about 45 ms, ignores acceleration below 0.15 m/s² when integrating, and integrates using the phone's monotonic sensor timestamps. A 3-second velocity damping constant and a reset after 0.65 seconds of quiet acceleration/gyro readings limit drift. Gaps above 0.2 seconds, invalid timestamps, pause/resume, calibration, and disconnection discard old velocity.

Quiet readings cannot distinguish a stationary phone from one traveling at constant speed. Consequently this estimate is suitable for brief hand gestures, not vehicle speed, long-duration tracking, or absolute position. Hold the phone still before starting or resetting the estimate. Heading may drift over long sessions; recalibrate as needed.


## Drawing Demo

Select **Drawing Demo** in the header. Connect the existing protocol-3 phone app, hold it in any comfortable steady grip (flat, inclined, or upright), and click **Set natural pose**. That grip becomes the virtual level for steering and the left-right trigger. The button requires fresh, unpaused motion and a reasonably steady hand; it has no screen-up requirement. Capturing a pose lifts the tip, stops motion, and resets the trigger while preserving the cursor position, artwork, tool, ink, sensitivity, and trigger settings. Screen-up holds still capture a default pose automatically after about 0.7 seconds when no pose has been set. After reconnection, upright users can capture their grip again with the button.

- Tilt left/right to move left/right. Tilt forward/backward to move up/down. Left plus forward moves diagonally **up-left**. Direction follows the combined tilt; larger angles move faster, with no extra diagonal speed boost.
- The virtual pen uses gravity projected onto the tilted plane, viscous damping, and sensor-time integration. Calibration aligns the control frame with gravity in the chosen grip, so an upright phone steers sideways by leaning left/right and forward/backward by tipping, while yaw about gravity does not steer. All steering and safety angles are measured relative to the captured pose. It accelerates smoothly toward an angle-dependent speed and brakes quickly when brought level. A 1.5° dead zone tolerates hand tremor. Travel stays inside the paper without accumulating hidden movement at its edges. **Tilt sensitivity** adjusts the response.
- From your natural pose, a quick **left tilt followed by a right tilt** presses the tip onto the paper; repeat to lift it. Both sides must cross the **Angle each side** threshold (8–45°, default 18°) within **Gesture time** (0.15–1.00 s, default 0.45 s), measured from leaving the chosen neutral grip until reaching the right threshold. Return to that grip briefly to rearm. Candidate and completed gestures suppress cursor movement/ink until they finish or time out; ordinary slow tilts still steer. The sliders apply to live control and preview, cancel a pending trigger safely, and retain their values through calibration/reconnect/mode switches. Shaking alone no longer toggles drawing. Light haptics distinguish putting the tip down from lifting it.
- Choose **Pen** for even ink, **Pencil** for softer textured strokes, or **Brush** for a broad stroke that narrows with movement speed. Six ink colors are available. Each stroke preserves its tool and color when selections change. These are virtual tool styles; the phone does not measure tip pressure.
- **Undo**, **Clear paper**, **Center tip**, a dot-grid toggle, and an emergency **Lift tip** control are available. The paper persists when switching demos within the open Mac app. Disconnecting, stale input, long sensor gaps, and leaving the view lift the tip to prevent stray connecting marks. Reconnection sets a fresh level before drawing resumes. Artwork is currently kept in memory until the app quits, with a 160,000-point limit.
- **Try demo** draws with simulated tilt through the same engine on a separate preview paper. **Stop demo** restores your own paper. The preview never sends phone haptics.

The drawing demo requires only the existing motion stream; the phone app and wire protocol need no update. `Shared/DrawingGameplay.swift` owns tilt physics, gesture detection, tools, and stroke data. `macOS/DrawingDemoView.swift` owns controller integration and Canvas rendering. New tools can add their own stroke profiles and renderers without adding protocol messages.

## Ladybug Laser Maze

Select **Maze Demo**. Pair the existing protocol-3 phone app and use **Set natural pose** with a comfortable steady grip, or hold the screen upward for automatic setup. Hold neutral briefly to start. Tilt left/right and forward/backward to move the ladybug left/right and up/down; diagonal tilts combine naturally and larger angles move faster. The maze shares `TiltPose` and `TiltPhysics` with Drawing Demo, including gravity-aligned upright grips, inertia, neutral braking, and sensor-time integration. No drawing toggle gesture is used. **Movement sensitivity** adjusts speed.

- Reach the green **EXIT** while keeping the entire ladybug clear of red lasers. The outer border is also lethal. A swept circle/capsule collision test checks the full path between samples, so fast movement cannot tunnel through walls or their rounded ends.
- A hit triggers a burn effect and failure haptic once. After 0.85 seconds the bug returns to **START**, the attempt count increases, and holding neutral for 0.25 seconds rearms movement. A held tilt cannot immediately send the respawned bug into a wall.
- The display tracks laser hits, attempts, speed, and active elapsed time. Reaching EXIT freezes the run and gives a success effect/haptic. A winner sheet asks for a player name (up to 24 characters) before saving the frozen time and laser-hit count. **Skip** dismisses it without saving; **Save time** lets an unsaved winner reopen the form later. Duplicate submissions from the same run cannot add another entry. Preview wins never open the name prompt or enter the leaderboard. **Restart run** clears the current run; **New maze** generates a different solvable layout. Both preserve the current calibrated grip and sensitivity.
- The **Leaderboard** is saved locally in `Application Support/MotionLink/maze-leaderboard.json` using atomic writes. **This maze** ranks the current layout by fastest time; **All mazes** shows saved records across layouts, marked when they belong to another maze. Times display hundredths of a second. It shows the top 10, retains the fastest 100 per layout and up to 2,000 records overall, and survives restarting the app. Names support Unicode and are trimmed to one line. Save failures keep the name form open for retry; an unreadable existing archive is not silently overwritten.
- The 8×6 maze is generated as a spanning tree with a guaranteed START-to-EXIT route. No solution line is shown during play. **Watch demo** uses simulated motion through the same engine to demonstrate a burn, respawn, and guided escape on a separate preview run; it preserves the user's run and emits no controller haptics.
- Paused/stale/disconnected input freezes motion and requires setup plus a neutral hold before resuming. Recalibrating preserves progress and laser-hit counts. Leaving and returning to the view preserves the run within the open Mac app.

The demo uses the existing motion stream and needs no iPhone app or protocol update. Its generation/collision/game logic lives in `Shared/LaserMazeGameplay.swift`, reusable tilt controls in `Shared/TiltControls.swift`, and Mac UI/controller routing in `macOS/LaserMazeView.swift`.

## Paper Plane Flight

Select **Paper Plane**. Pair the existing protocol-3 iPhone app, hold your comfortable grip, and click **Set natural pose**. A steady screen-up hold also calibrates automatically. Click **Launch flight**, then hold neutral for a relaxed three-second countdown. Movement or an interrupted sensor stream resets the countdown.

- Lean left/right to bank and turn. A larger bank turns faster; return to neutral to straighten the wings while preserving your heading. Tip the phone forward to dive and backward to climb. Upright grips use the same gravity-aligned controls as Drawing and Maze. **Steering response** adjusts bank/pitch gain.
- Push the phone forward quickly to trigger a 1.6-second boost. The acceleration is measured in your calibrated grip's gravity-aligned forward direction; a backward push does not boost. Relax before another boost, with a 2.5-second recharge.
- Thread eight rings through a winding 3D course above the sea. A safe pass scores 100 points; passing near the center scores 150. Rings turn green or gold after a pass, and gray after a miss. The next ring is gold with a center beacon. The HUD shows virtual airspeed, altitude, rings passed, and score.
- Avoid the ring rims, dark storm clouds, and water. Continuous swept collision checks use a conservative envelope around the nose and wing tips. Blue wind pockets gently buffet the plane. A stable chase camera follows behind while keeping the horizon level, and twin wingtip trails show the path. Reduce Motion suppresses the trails and crash spin.
- Live play sends controller feedback for launch, boost, crosswind entry, ring passes/misses, crashes, and completion. **Fly again** restarts the course using your current grip. Leaving the view, pausing, or disconnecting suspends control; a fresh pose and brief neutral hold are required to resume, preserving flight position and score.
- **Watch demo** guides a separate plane through all eight rings, including a boost, using synthetic motion through the actual flight engine. It preserves your live flight and emits no controller haptics. Stop the preview and use **Set natural pose** to resume your own flight.

This is an assisted gliding game: gravity-aligned bank creates coordinated turns, pitch changes climb/dive and target airspeed, and both attitude and airspeed respond smoothly on the sensor clock with 120 Hz substeps. It does not estimate the phone's world position or claim to simulate full paper aerodynamics. Displayed speed and altitude belong to the virtual plane. The iPhone camera is not needed, and neither the phone app nor the protocol needs an update. Logic lives in `Shared/PaperFlightGameplay.swift`, controller/UI state in `macOS/PaperFlightView.swift`, and the programmatic RealityKit scene in `macOS/PaperFlightArenaView.swift`.

## Connection

Bonjour discovers `_motionlink._tcp` receivers. Apple's Network framework transports length-prefixed JSON over local TCP, with TCP_NODELAY enabled. Each motion payload contains a protocol version, sequence number, sensor timestamp, quaternion, angular velocity, user acceleration, and gravity.

The stream allows at most one pending motion send, dropping samples under backpressure. The receiver checks frame lengths, JSON, protocol version, quaternion norms, finite sensor values, and sequence ordering. Pairing and connection attempts have deadlines; stale callbacks cannot affect a replacement connection.

The six-digit code provides an application-level pairing gate. This version uses local TCP **without transport encryption**; it is intended for your own trusted network. No sensor data is stored or uploaded to a server.

Protocol version **3** adds bidirectional game-controller haptics: capability negotiation, custom event/envelope playback, finite looping, live parameter updates, stop-one/stop-all, and correlated acknowledgements/lifecycle reports. Update **both apps** when upgrading from protocol 2 and re-pair; protocol-2 builds are incompatible. See [Controller haptics API](Docs/ControllerHaptics.md) for Swift usage, wire schema, limits, and lifecycle behavior. The phone uses Core Haptics, with a controller-side enable toggle. Expand **Controller feedback** in the Mac pairing card to test taps, success cues, and rumble. Sword hits, Wok toss/landing/result events, drawing tip toggles, maze burn/respawn/escape, and paper plane flight events also provide feedback during live play.

Both apps declare local-network and Bonjour usage. The Mac is sandboxed with incoming and outgoing network entitlements.

## Physical-device acceptance check

1. Pair the devices. Confirm that only the paired phone can stream to the Mac.
2. Set neutral while upright. Tilt the top toward/away from you, turn left/right, and rotate clockwise/counterclockwise; check the model follows each axis in the same direction.
3. Rotate past 180°; the model should remain continuous and show its rear cameras.
4. Move the phone right/left, up/down, and toward/away from the neutral screen. Check the direction highlights and green arrow. On braking, check the amber acceleration arrow reverses while the movement estimate may remain forward. Hold still and reset speed on either app.
5. Recalibrate at a different pose; check it becomes the new upright model pose.
6. Open Sword Demo. Start with a flat or moving phone; the confirmation button must remain unavailable. Hold upright and steady, explicitly confirm, and check the sword points upward. Tilt forward/backward and combine yaw/roll; verify it follows the full phone attitude in depth. Move the hand left/right, up/down, and toward/away while retaining orientation; verify the grip translates. Make edge-first sweeps and check each cut follows the blade angle.
7. Select **Reset neutral** and verify slicing pauses until another steady hold and confirmation. Pause/resume and verify a new confirmation is required.

## Files

- `Shared/`: protocol, quaternion math, TCP connection, motion estimation, neutral-posture monitoring, sword controls/collision logic, and common UI components.
- `iOS/`: discovery, sensor lifecycle, and phone interface.
- `macOS/`: receiver, telemetry interface, sword and wok arenas, 2D drawing studio, laser maze, paper plane flight, and programmatic RealityKit models.
- `Tests/`: Swift Testing unit and transport tests.
- `Scripts/generate_project.py`: regenerates the checked-in Xcode project and Info.plists while preserving existing signing settings.
- `Scripts/generate_icons.swift`: generates the app icon asset catalogs.
- `Scripts/verify_receiver.swift`: exercises the production receiver over loopback TCP.

API references: [Core Motion processed data](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/getting-processed-device-motion-data), [Network listener](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/network/nwlistener), and [RealityKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/realitykit).

Wok contact-model references: [MIT’s Newtonian mechanics and friction notes](https://live.ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/mit8_01scs22_chapter8.pdf). The simulation uses world coordinates so rotating-frame inertial effects arise from the moving contact surface rather than additional fictitious-force terms. The egg is approximated as a rigid contact body; deformation and oil fluid dynamics are not modeled.
